What I actually built

The two production systems I own at HiAcuity, drawn honestly — including the parts that exist only because the naive version didn't hold up.

01

Multi-tenant candidate search

100K+ profiles · sub-second · MongoDB Atlas + Voyage

inputRecruiter querynatural language
LangGraphReasoning stepsplits hard constraints from intent
MongoDBStructured filtersyears · skills · location · tenant
Atlas VectorSemantic searchchunked, max-pooled → 1 vector per profile
Voyage AIRerank shortlistnot the corpus
outputRanked candidates
  • Why max-pool the chunks? One candidate should be one result. Chunk-level scoring put ten fragments of the same resume in the top ten.
  • Why split the query first? Hard constraints are cheap in Mongo and expensive in a vector index. Filtering before search is what keeps it sub-second — reranking the whole corpus never would have.
  • Where tenancy lives. At the filter layer, not the application layer, so a missing check can't leak another tenant's candidates.
02

AI interview & assessment agent

LangGraph state machine · live transcription · auditable scoring

RecallLive interviewWebSocket transcription
LangGraphCompetency blockstate machine, not a prompt chain
criticFairness criticscreens follow-ups for bias before they're asked
validateClaim checkspoken claims vs. the candidate's resume
storeStructured assessmentcomparable across candidates
  • Why a state machine? Follow-ups stay scoped to the competency being assessed, and the whole run is replayable — a flat prompt chain is neither.
  • Why a critic node? A biased follow-up is unrecoverable once a candidate has heard it. Screening happens before it's asked, not in review.
  • Why structured scores? Free-text assessments can't be compared. Structured ones can be audited, aggregated, and challenged.
